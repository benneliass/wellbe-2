## Question

For "Ask WellBe" (the answer engine behind the Home entry point WEL-157), how should free-text questions be answered safely?
1. **Grounding scope** — what data an answer may draw on (the user's own threads/graph only, source-linked) and how out-of-corpus knowledge is handled.
2. **C10 gating** — do-not-diagnose, never-alarm, provenance, and bias enforcement on generated answers.
3. **Provenance/citation contract** — how each answer cites the user's own sources.
4. **Out-of-scope / uncertain-question handling** — refusal/redirect semantics, and when to route to triage or a clinician.

## Context

Touches C10 (Safety & Governance Gate — mandatory before any user-facing AI output) plus C7/C6/C5 (threads, graph, provenance) and likely C14 (Investigation). The "Ask WellBe" entry point is already shipped (WEL-157), so an unresearched answer engine is the highest-risk gap in the build-out: a user can already type a question into live UI. See `docs/architecture/component-map.md`, `docs/safety/safety_model.md`, `docs/safety/do_not_diagnose_rules.md`.

# Track G: Ask WellBe answer engine research brief

Scope note: This brief is decision-neutral. It summarizes external patterns and evidence relevant to the Ask WellBe answer engine, grounding scope, C10 safety gating, provenance, and out-of-scope or uncertain handling. It does not select an architecture, policy, model, or clinical workflow. Citations are inline Markdown links to sources consulted during this research.

## External patterns to examine

### 1. Grounding scope and retrieval-augmented generation over personal health data

**Closed-corpus RAG.** Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a common pattern for reducing reliance on a model's latent knowledge by retrieving documents from a corpus and conditioning generation on those retrieved passages. The original RAG paper describes combining a parametric model with a non-parametric memory accessed through a retriever, and reports more specific, diverse, and factual generated language than a parametric-only baseline in its experiments ([Lewis et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/6b493230205f780e1bc26945df7481e5-Abstract.html)). For WellBe, this pattern maps to a corpus boundary question: C7 Health Threads, C6 graph nodes/edges, and C5 evidence objects can be treated as a private retrieval corpus, while any general medical knowledge would be a separate corpus with distinct authority, versioning, and citation semantics.

**RAG is not a complete hallucination control.** RAG evaluation literature separates retrieval quality from answer faithfulness. RAGAS frames RAG evaluation around retriever context relevance, faithful use of retrieved passages, and generation quality, including settings without ground-truth answers ([Es et al., EACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/)). RAGTruth documents that RAG systems can still produce unsupported or contradictory claims relative to retrieved references ([Wu et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/)). These sources support examining RAG as one layer of grounding rather than as the full safety mechanism.

**Closed personal corpus versus layered personal plus general corpus.** A closed personal corpus aligns with a source-linked, personal-first product identity because every answerable claim can be tied to the user's own records, threads, graph facts, and evidence. A layered corpus can add general educational context, but it introduces additional design questions: which medical sources are allowed, whether general sources can be used when personal evidence is absent, how personal and general citations are distinguished, and how the system prevents general knowledge from being phrased as a user-specific conclusion. FDA clinical decision support guidance emphasizes that users of certain CDS software must be able to independently review the basis for recommendations rather than relying primarily on the software output ([FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)).

**Retrieval security and prompt-injection controls.** RAG over documents creates a security boundary because retrieved text can contain adversarial instructions or misleading content. OWASP's LLM guidance identifies prompt injection, including indirect injection through external content, as a critical LLM application risk, and lists mitigations such as constraining model behavior, validating output formats, least privilege, human approval for high-risk actions, segregating external content, and adversarial testing ([OWASP LLM Top 10](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/); [OWASP Prompt Injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). For Ask WellBe, the external pattern is to treat user-uploaded records, thread notes, and retrieved snippets as untrusted evidence content, not as instructions.

### 2. Safety gating and guardrails for health LLM output

**Risk-management lifecycle rather than one prompt.** NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is intended to help organizations manage risks to individuals, organizations, and society from AI systems and to improve trustworthiness across design, development, use, and evaluation ([NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)). NIST's Generative AI Profile adapts the AI RMF to generative AI risks and risk-management actions ([NIST AI 600-1 Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)). This pattern frames C10 not as a disclaimer or prompt fragment, but as a lifecycle control point with pre-release, runtime, monitoring, and incident-response implications.

**Health-specific governance and assurance.** WHO's 2024 guidance on large multi-modal models in health addresses generative AI systems that can process and generate multiple data types and discusses ethics and governance issues for health, public health, scientific, and drug-development uses ([WHO LMM guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240084759)). CHAI's Blueprint for Trustworthy AI Implementation Guidance and Assurance for Healthcare presents healthcare AI assurance recommendations developed with health systems, academia, government, and industry participants ([CHAI Blueprint](https://www.chai.org/workgroup/responsible-ai/blueprint-for-trustworthy-ai)). The National Academy of Medicine's AI Code of Conduct for Health and Medicine identifies persistent concerns including bias, equity and access, privacy and security, accuracy and reliability, explainability, transparency, accountability, automation, and anthropomorphism ([NAM AI Code of Conduct executive summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29087/chapter/2)). These sources support examining C10 as a clinical-safety, privacy, equity, and accountability gate, not only a content-moderation gate.

**Bias and equity controls.** The Health AI Partnership's HEAAL framework organizes equity review across accountability, fairness, fitness for purpose, reliability and validity, and transparency ([Health AI Partnership HEAAL](https://healthaipartnership.org/health-equity-across-the-ai-lifecycle-heaal)). NAM also calls out standardized assessment and reporting of bias in data, AI output, and AI use ([NAM AI Code of Conduct executive summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29087/chapter/2)). For Ask WellBe, this external pattern maps to questions about whether the gate checks answer language, source coverage, demographic assumptions, and model performance across user groups.

**Boundary between wellness, education, CDS, and medical device claims.** FDA's general wellness policy covers low-risk products intended for a healthy lifestyle and notes statutory exclusions for some software that maintains or encourages a healthy lifestyle and is unrelated to diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of disease or condition ([FDA General Wellness Policy](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices)). FDA's CDS guidance discusses software functions that analyze patient or medical information and provide recommendations about prevention, diagnosis, or treatment, and explains criteria for non-device CDS intended for health care professionals, including independent review of the recommendation basis ([FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). These sources are regulatory evidence to examine, not legal conclusions about WellBe.

**Medical LLM risk evidence.** Recent medical-AI literature reports limitations in LLM diagnostic and clinical reasoning tasks. A Nature Medicine study found that evaluated state-of-the-art LLMs did not accurately diagnose across all pathologies, performed worse than physicians, and struggled with guideline-following and lab interpretation in the studied settings ([Kanjee et al., Nature Medicine 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03097-1)). A JAMA systematic review of 519 studies on LLM applications in health care found that only a small share used real patient-care data, highlighting evaluation limitations across the field ([JAMA 2024 systematic review](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2825147)). A 2026 JAMA Network Open study evaluated 21 LLMs across standardized clinical vignettes and found variation by reasoning task, with differential diagnosis performance lower than several other task categories in that evaluation ([JAMA Network Open 2026](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847679)). These sources support examining conservative, non-diagnostic gating for an open-ended consumer-facing answer surface.

### 3. Provenance, citation, and attribution contracts for grounded answers

**Attribution to identified sources.** Attributable-to-identified-sources (AIS) is an evaluation framework for natural-language generation in which output claims about the external world are assessed against an independent provided source. The AIS paper frames attribution as a way to support safety and reliability when generated text is used in knowledge-intensive settings ([Rashkin et al., Computational Linguistics 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/)). For WellBe, AIS is an external pattern for testing C5's "no orphan claims" principle at the claim level.

**Citation quality is measurable but imperfect.** ALCE introduces a benchmark and metrics for generating answers with citations, covering fluency, correctness, and citation quality; it reports that even strong systems can fail to provide complete citation support on long-form QA tasks ([Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)). This pattern separates citation presence from citation support: a visible link is not the same as proof that the cited source entails the claim.

**Structured provenance lineage.** The W3C PROV family describes provenance as information about entities, activities, and people involved in producing data or things, useful for assessing quality, reliability, and trustworthiness, and provides a model for interoperable provenance exchange ([W3C PROV Overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)). This maps naturally to C5 evidence objects, C7 thread entries, C6 graph edges, retrieval activities, generation activities, and C10 gate verdicts as distinct provenance entities and activities.

**Independent review basis.** FDA's CDS guidance repeatedly emphasizes the ability of the intended user to independently review the basis for a recommendation in non-device CDS criteria for HCP-oriented software ([FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). Although the guidance is not a product-design template for a consumer assistant, it provides an external pattern for exposing enough basis, source, and logic that a human can understand why an answer was produced.

### 4. Refusal, hedging, uncertainty, and escalation for consumer health assistants

**Non-diagnostic triage pattern.** NHS 111 Online states that it tells users what to do next and does not give a diagnosis ([NHS 111 Online](https://111.nhs.uk/)). NHS Digital describes NHS 111 Online as using clinical content to advise next steps and timeframes rather than giving a diagnosis ([NHS Digital: NHS 111 Online as a Class 1 medical device](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-111-online/nhs-111-online-is-a-class-1-medical-device)). Healthdirect Australia's symptom checker similarly states that it cannot give a diagnosis and is not a substitute for professional health care, while helping users understand symptoms and when to seek medical attention ([healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/symptom-checker)). These are established examples of consumer-facing, non-diagnostic health information tools that route users to next steps.

**Emergency redirect pattern.** Healthdirect's government page tells users to call emergency services immediately in an emergency before using the symptom checker ([Australian Government: healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/apps-and-tools/healthdirect-symptom-checker)). MedlinePlus states that getting medical help right away in a medical emergency can save a life and lists warning-sign categories ([MedlinePlus Medical Emergencies](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm)). NINDS provides urgent emergency-service instructions for time-sensitive neurological warning signs ([NINDS Signs and Symptoms](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/stroke/signs-and-symptoms)). These sources illustrate the pattern of calm, specific escalation to emergency care when risk is high, rather than an alarm-only message.

**Uncertainty handling pattern.** The same triage and attribution sources imply separate uncertain states: the system may be unable to answer because the question is out of scope, because retrieved personal evidence is insufficient, because sources conflict, because the request asks for diagnosis or treatment, or because the content requires urgent human assessment. The answer behavior differs across these states: refuse, hedge, ask for structured triage information, route to an investigation, or direct to a clinician/emergency service. The sources do not define a single threshold for these choices; they provide categories and examples to examine.

## Decision-neutral findings

### DQ1 - Grounding scope: what may an answer draw on?

**Finding 1.1 - RAG supports grounded QA, but source scope remains a product policy boundary.** External RAG literature supports the technical pattern of retrieving relevant documents and conditioning generation on them, but it does not decide whether the allowed corpus is closed personal data, general medical knowledge, or both ([Lewis et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/6b493230205f780e1bc26945df7481e5-Abstract.html)). In WellBe component terms, C7 threads, C6 graph nodes/edges, and C5 evidence objects can form a closed personal corpus. A separate curated medical corpus would be a distinct evidence source with different authority, update cadence, and risk profile. The decision point is not whether RAG exists; it is which corpora are in scope for which question classes.

**Finding 1.2 - Closed-corpus personal retrieval maximizes user-specific source linkage, but may increase non-answer and uncertainty cases.** A personal-only corpus can enforce the principle that every user-specific claim is grounded in the individual's own data. It also means the engine may be unable to answer general medical questions or explain concepts not present in the user's sources. Attribution literature supports verifying generated claims against identified sources, but does not require every answer system to use only a single corpus ([Rashkin et al., Computational Linguistics 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/)). The resulting design question is how often Ask WellBe should say, in effect, "I cannot answer that from your sources," versus drawing on external educational material.

**Finding 1.3 - Adding general medical knowledge creates a source-separation problem.** General medical knowledge can provide educational context, but it can also make an answer sound more clinically authoritative than the user's data supports. FDA CDS guidance's independent-review-basis concept is relevant here because it emphasizes exposing the basis for a recommendation rather than asking the user to rely primarily on opaque software output ([FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). For Ask WellBe, the source-separation issue is whether a sentence is grounded in the user's personal evidence, in a general reference, in both, or in neither.

**Finding 1.4 - RAG needs retrieval-sufficiency and answer-faithfulness checks.** RAGAS treats retrieval relevance, faithful use of retrieved context, and generation quality as separable evaluation targets ([Es et al., EACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/)). RAGTruth shows that retrieved context does not prevent unsupported or contradictory answer claims ([Wu et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/)). A C10 gating contract for Ask WellBe therefore has an evidence basis in external literature for checking both the retrieved evidence and the generated answer, rather than only checking whether retrieval ran.

**Finding 1.5 - Prompt-injection risk applies to personal health sources.** In RAG, retrieved notes, imported documents, messages, or OCR text may include instructions or adversarial content. OWASP identifies indirect prompt injection through external content as an LLM application risk and describes RAG-specific scenarios where compromised documents can mislead responses ([OWASP Prompt Injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)). For WellBe, this affects C7, C6, and C5 because corpus items are evidence, not instructions; it also affects C10 because the gate can be asked to verify that retrieved source text did not override system behavior.

**Affected components.** C7 is the natural primary retrieval surface for thread-local questions. C6 can support graph-aware retrieval, relationship traversal, and evidence-weighted edges, but graph edges such as `may_explain` still require hedging and source linkage. C5 supplies source objects, evidence spans, and citation identifiers. C10 determines whether retrieved evidence is sufficient and whether the generated answer remains non-diagnostic and source-linked. C14 may be involved when the answer cannot close the user's question and instead opens or continues an investigation.

### DQ2 - Safety gating: how should generated answers enforce non-diagnosis, never-alarm language, provenance, and bias control?

**Finding 2.1 - External governance sources frame safety as layered and continuous.** NIST AI RMF, NIST's Generative AI Profile, WHO's LMM health guidance, CHAI's blueprint, and NAM's AI Code of Conduct all treat AI risk management as broader than prompt engineering or post-hoc disclaimers ([NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework); [NIST AI 600-1](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence); [WHO LMM guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240084759); [CHAI Blueprint](https://www.chai.org/workgroup/responsible-ai/blueprint-for-trustworthy-ai); [NAM AI Code of Conduct executive summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29087/chapter/2)). These sources support examining C10 as a layered contract that can include input classification, retrieval checks, generation constraints, output validation, logging, monitoring, and incident review.

**Finding 2.2 - Non-diagnostic consumer health tools commonly route to next steps rather than final answers.** NHS 111 Online explicitly presents itself as telling users what to do next and not giving a diagnosis ([NHS 111 Online](https://111.nhs.uk/)). Healthdirect's symptom checker states that it cannot diagnose and is not a substitute for professional health care ([healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/symptom-checker)). These examples are not LLM answer engines, but they are concrete external patterns for separating user-facing health navigation from diagnosis.

**Finding 2.3 - Medical LLM evaluations support caution around diagnostic and treatment-like outputs.** Peer-reviewed evaluations report material limitations in LLM diagnostic and clinical reasoning performance, including diagnostic accuracy and performance differences by clinical task ([Kanjee et al., Nature Medicine 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03097-1); [JAMA Network Open 2026](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847679)). The JAMA systematic review also indicates that much health-care LLM evaluation has limited real patient-care data exposure, which is relevant to deployment claims ([JAMA 2024 systematic review](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2825147)). For C10, these sources are evidence for treating diagnosis, differential diagnosis, management, treatment, and lab-interpretation requests as high-risk categories requiring explicit policy decisions.

**Finding 2.4 - Provenance can be a gate condition, not only a display feature.** The AIS framework evaluates whether generated claims are supported by identified sources ([Rashkin et al., Computational Linguistics 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/)). ALCE shows that citation quality is measurable and that citation support can be incomplete even when citations appear in generated answers ([Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)). For C10, this supports examining a release condition such as "all non-trivial claims have supporting C5 citations and pass a support check," while leaving the exact threshold and claim taxonomy undecided.

**Finding 2.5 - Bias controls need lifecycle and answer-level treatment.** NAM calls out bias, equity and access, explainability, transparency, accountability, privacy and security, and user-centered design as concerns for health AI ([NAM AI Code of Conduct executive summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29087/chapter/2)). HEAAL organizes equity evaluation around accountability, fairness, fitness for purpose, reliability/validity, and transparency ([Health AI Partnership HEAAL](https://healthaipartnership.org/health-equity-across-the-ai-lifecycle-heaal)). For Ask WellBe, bias can enter through the user's data completeness, retrieval ranking, graph-edge weighting, general medical sources, answer phrasing, urgency routing, and model behavior. The external sources do not define a single bias-control algorithm; they define categories for evaluation and governance.

**Finding 2.6 - Security guardrails overlap with clinical safety.** OWASP's LLM risks include prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, excessive agency, and overreliance ([OWASP LLM Top 10](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/)). For Ask WellBe, a prompt-injected source could cause a privacy leak, a non-grounded answer, or an unsafe care instruction. The distinction between security gate and clinical safety gate may therefore be operational rather than absolute.

**Affected components.** C10 is the release gate for every generated answer. C5 provides the evidence map C10 can check. C7 and C6 affect safety because retrieval and graph weighting determine what the model sees as context. C14 may be used when the safe output is not a direct answer but an investigation path, structured follow-up, or triage check-in.

### DQ3 - Provenance and citation contract: how should each answer cite the user's own sources?

**Finding 3.1 - Claim support and citation presence are different requirements.** AIS asks whether generated content is attributable to identified sources; ALCE measures citation quality and reports that generated citations can be incomplete even in strong systems ([Rashkin et al., Computational Linguistics 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/); [Gao et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.398/)). For WellBe, this maps to two separable C5/C10 checks: whether a claim has a citation object, and whether the cited evidence actually supports the claim.

**Finding 3.2 - Provenance can cover the full answer-production chain.** W3C PROV models provenance through entities, activities, and agents ([W3C PROV Overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)). In Ask WellBe terms, possible provenance entities include source documents, user-entered notes, imported lab reports, thread entries, graph nodes, graph edges, retrieved snippets, generated drafts, C10 verdicts, and final answer blocks. Possible provenance activities include ingestion, extraction, graph-edge creation, retrieval, generation, citation assignment, safety gating, and answer rendering. This does not decide which of these must be exposed to the user; it defines an external vocabulary for traceability.

**Finding 3.3 - The FDA independent-review-basis concept is relevant to citation UX.** FDA CDS guidance discusses enabling independent review of the recommendation basis for certain HCP-oriented non-device CDS criteria ([FDA Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). Although Ask WellBe is described as non-diagnostic and consumer-oriented, the pattern is relevant to source-linked answer design: one design variable is how answer readers can inspect the basis for a user-facing assertion without treating the system as a black-box medical authority.

**Finding 3.4 - Citation granularity is an unresolved design dimension.** External citation benchmarks evaluate source support at relatively fine granularity, while consumer UIs often show paragraph-level or item-level citations. Claim-level citations maximize auditability but can overload a calm consumer interface. Paragraph-level citations reduce clutter but can hide orphan clauses. Source cards can increase user control but may not prove claim-level entailment. None of the consulted sources mandates a single granularity for a personal health answer engine.

**Finding 3.5 - Personal and general citations need distinguishable semantics if both are allowed.** If the engine can draw on both user-specific evidence and general medical references, a citation contract must distinguish personal-source support from background educational support. The need follows from attribution and independent-review-basis patterns: a general citation can support a general statement, but it cannot by itself support a user-specific inference about the individual's records ([Rashkin et al., Computational Linguistics 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.cl-4.2/); [FDA CDS guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)).

**Affected components.** C5 is the authoritative owner of source objects, evidence spans, provenance metadata, and no-orphan-claim enforcement. C7 and C6 are affected because source IDs can be preserved when thread entries and graph edges are created or retrieved. C10 can validate citation completeness and support before release. The Ask answer renderer decides how much of the provenance chain is visible inline, in source cards, or in an expandable evidence view.

### DQ4 - Out-of-scope and uncertain handling: when should the engine refuse, hedge, or redirect?

**Finding 4.1 - Consumer health triage tools commonly state a non-diagnostic scope and provide next-step routing.** NHS 111 Online and healthdirect are external examples of consumer-facing health tools that do not give diagnoses and instead direct users toward appropriate next steps ([NHS 111 Online](https://111.nhs.uk/); [NHS Digital: NHS 111 Online as a Class 1 medical device](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-111-online/nhs-111-online-is-a-class-1-medical-device); [healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/symptom-checker)). For Ask WellBe, this supports examining refusal and redirect behavior as first-class answer modes rather than failures.

**Finding 4.2 - Emergency escalation is a separate state from ordinary uncertainty.** Public health sources instruct users to seek immediate emergency help for emergency warning signs, and symptom-checker pages tell users not to use the checker first in emergencies ([Australian Government: healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/apps-and-tools/healthdirect-symptom-checker); [MedlinePlus Medical Emergencies](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm); [NINDS Signs and Symptoms](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/stroke/signs-and-symptoms)). This supports separating ordinary "not enough evidence" from high-urgency input where the safe response is calm, direct escalation.

**Finding 4.3 - Several uncertain states require different output behavior.** The external evidence suggests at least these distinct cases: (a) evidence-insufficient personal question; (b) source conflict; (c) request for diagnosis, treatment, medication change, or clinical management; (d) potentially urgent symptom report; (e) out-of-domain request; (f) privacy/security concern; and (g) prompt-injection or unsafe retrieved content. RAGAS and RAGTruth cover retrieval and faithfulness failure modes ([Es et al., EACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/); [Wu et al., ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/)). NHS 111 and healthdirect cover non-diagnostic next-step routing ([NHS 111 Online](https://111.nhs.uk/); [healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/symptom-checker)). OWASP covers prompt-injection and unsafe external-content failure modes ([OWASP Prompt Injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)).

**Finding 4.4 - Hedging is not a substitute for scope control.** Product language can hedge uncertain statements, but the medical LLM evaluation literature shows that clinical reasoning and diagnostic tasks themselves can be unreliable in studied settings ([Kanjee et al., Nature Medicine 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03097-1); [JAMA Network Open 2026](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2847679)). The external evidence therefore distinguishes hedged explanation of source-linked facts from hedged diagnosis or treatment advice. The latter remains a separate policy boundary.

**Finding 4.5 - Investigation routing is an option distinct from answering or refusing.** WellBe's operating loop and C14 Investigation Engine create an internal answer mode that the external triage tools do not directly cover: the system can acknowledge that the current sources do not support a grounded answer and route toward a structured investigation or clarification path. The closest external analogs are triage systems that collect structured information before advising next steps ([NHS Digital: NHS 111 Online as a Class 1 medical device](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-111-online/nhs-111-online-is-a-class-1-medical-device); [healthdirect Symptom Checker](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/symptom-checker)). Whether Ask WellBe should open C14 automatically, ask user permission, or only suggest it is a product decision.

**Affected components.** C10 classifies urgent, diagnostic, prescriptive, out-of-domain, and evidence-insufficient requests. C5 determines whether the system can support a claim. C7/C6 determine whether personal context exists for a grounded response. C14 can receive uncertain cases that need structured investigation rather than a direct answer. The Ask UI controls how refusal, hedge, redirect, and investigation states are presented calmly.

## Approaches considered

Grounded only in the recorded research:

- **Grounding scope.** RAG (Lewis et al.) grounds generation in retrieved documents but does not decide the corpus boundary; closed personal corpus (C7/C6/C5) vs. layered personal+general medical knowledge is a product policy. RAGAS/RAGTruth show retrieval ≠ faithfulness (unsupported claims occur even with RAG). OWASP indirect prompt injection means retrieved personal content is untrusted.
- **Safety gating.** NIST AI RMF + GenAI Profile, WHO LMM, CHAI, NAM, HEAAL frame health-AI safety as a layered lifecycle control, not a disclaimer. Medical-LLM evaluations (Kanjee/Nature Medicine; JAMA review; JAMA Network Open 2026) show diagnostic/clinical-reasoning unreliability → caution for diagnosis/treatment/management. NHS 111 / healthdirect model non-diagnostic next-step routing.
- **Provenance/citation.** AIS (attributable-to-identified-sources) and ALCE separate citation *presence* from citation *support*; W3C PROV models the full answer-production chain; FDA CDS "independent review of basis" supports inspectable evidence.
- **Out-of-scope/uncertain.** Triage tools treat refusal/redirect as first-class; emergency escalation is a distinct state; uncertainty has several types (evidence-insufficient, source conflict, diagnosis/treatment request, urgent, out-of-domain, prompt-injection); hedging is not a substitute for scope control.

## Decision

1. **Grounding scope = closed personal corpus first.** v1 RAG retrieves only over C7 threads + C6 graph (with hedged `may_explain`) + C5 evidence spans. **No general medical knowledge and no model latent medical knowledge** in v1 — the model synthesizes language over the user's own retrieved evidence only. Questions not answerable from the user's sources get an honest *"I can't answer that from your sources"* + redirect, not a general medical answer. All retrieved content (notes, imported docs, OCR) is treated as untrusted **evidence, never instructions** (prompt-injection segregation).
2. **Safety gating (C10) = a layered release gate, not a disclaimer.** (a) input intent pre-classification (diagnosis / treatment / management / medication / urgent / out-of-domain / evidence-insufficient); (b) retrieval-sufficiency check; (c) generation constrained to retrieved evidence; (d) output validation = non-diagnostic policy (no disease assertion or probability, no treatment/medication directive) + claim-level citation-support check + never-alarm language; (e) audit logging + monitoring. Diagnosis/treatment/management/medication requests are **out of scope → safe redirect**. Urgent input is a separate state → calm, specific escalation reusing the Track C tiers and locale emergency numbers.
3. **Provenance/citation = no orphan claims.** Every user-specific claim must have a C5 citation object **and** pass a support check (presence ≠ support). Granularity = **per user-specific claim**, rendered as inline source chips + an expandable evidence view. The per-answer audit object stores retrieval inputs, the draft, C10 verdicts, and final text (full PROV chain is audit-only). No claim is released without supporting evidence.
4. **Out-of-scope/uncertain = first-class answer modes:** source-grounded answer; soft refusal stating what *can* be answered from sources; clarify; redirect to clinician; calm emergency escalation; or route to a **C14 investigation**. C14 writes are **suggest-by-default** (user must confirm) and are themselves provenance-tracked and C10-gated. Source conflicts are surfaced, not silently resolved.

## Trade-offs accepted

- **Personal-only corpus** increases "can't answer from your sources" cases and may feel less helpful — accepted to keep every claim source-linked and prevent general knowledge masquerading as user-specific inference.
- **Claim-level citation + support check** adds latency and some false rejections — accepted to honor the no-orphan-claims principle and AIS/ALCE evidence that citations can be unsupported.
- **Conservative diagnostic-intent gating** may over-block some benign questions — accepted (calm redirect, never a silent diagnostic answer), consistent with medical-LLM reliability evidence.

## Implementation notes

- **C10** is the release gate (input class → retrieval-sufficiency → output validation → audit); this is a **core (C10)** decision, so dependent implementation Stories stay blocked on this record.
- **C5** owns citation objects, the claim-support check, and no-orphan enforcement; retrieval is hybrid over C7/C6/C5 with source-id preservation.
- **C14** receives uncertain/insufficient cases as suggested investigations requiring user confirmation.
- All retrieved text is handled as data, not instructions (OWASP indirect-injection mitigations).
- **Deferred (tracked):** exact thresholds (evidence sufficiency, escalation false-pos/neg, claim-granularity edge cases), any future curated general corpus (separate decision), and regulatory classification (legal review).
