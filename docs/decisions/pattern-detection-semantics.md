# Track E Research Brief: Non-diagnostic pattern-detection semantics over a personal health graph

Scope: This brief is decision-neutral. It summarizes external standards, methods, and evidence relevant to temporal precedence, co-occurrence/pattern definition, and confidence/uncertainty surfacing for a read/annotate-only, source-linked, non-diagnostic pattern view. It does not choose an architecture, policy, threshold, label set, or scoring formula.

## External patterns to examine

### 1. Event-time semantics, data lineage, and quality metadata

- **Separate clinically relevant time from record/availability time.** HL7 FHIR Observation distinguishes `effective[x]` as the clinically relevant time or period for an observation from `issued`, the time the observation version was made available; the FHIR page also notes that Observation is not intended to record clinical diagnosis, which is typically represented by Condition or ClinicalImpression ([HL7 FHIR Observation R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/observation.html)). This pattern is directly relevant to WellBe's temporal-precedence question because recorded timestamp order, clinically relevant event time, and source-entry time can differ.
- **Track provenance as context for trust, reliability, and reproducibility.** FHIR Provenance records the entities, agents, and activities involved in producing or influencing a resource and states that provenance can support assessments of authenticity, reliability, trustworthiness, and investigation of origins ([HL7 FHIR Provenance R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/provenance.html)). W3C PROV-O provides a domain-independent model for representing provenance information across systems ([W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). This pattern aligns with source-linked derived pattern annotations.
- **Treat data quality as task-dependent rather than inherent.** Weiskopf and Weng identify EHR data quality dimensions including completeness, correctness, concordance, plausibility, and currency, and emphasize that data quality is fitness-for-use and varies by intended purpose ([Weiskopf and Weng, 2013](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/20/1/144/2909176)). Kahn et al. harmonize secondary-use EHR data quality into conformance, completeness, and plausibility, explicitly aimed at understanding strengths and limitations of EHR data for analytics and research ([Kahn et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27713905/)).
- **Represent missingness explicitly where possible.** FHIR guidance for handling missing data distinguishes optional omitted data from required data whose absence should carry a data-absent reason; it also includes patterns for indicative temporal information when exact dates are unknown ([HL7 Europe Base and Core FHIR IG: Handling Missing Data](https://hl7.eu/fhir/base/missing-data.html)).

### 2. Temporal-precedence and causal-order caveats

- **Temporality is necessary for causality but not sufficient.** Epidemiology teaching materials based on Bradford Hill state that exposure must precede outcome, while also emphasizing that observed associations may be due to chance, bias, or confounding and that temporal sequence may be difficult to ascertain ([Health Knowledge: Causation in Epidemiology](https://www.healthknowledge.org.uk/e-learning/epidemiology/practitioners/causation-epidemiology-association-causation)).
- **User-reported onset/order is vulnerable to recall error and bias.** Coughlin's review of recall bias notes that inaccurate recall depends on exposure and respondent characteristics, and that study protocol and interviewing/questionnaire design affect recall quality ([Coughlin, 1990](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/recall-bias-in-epidemiologic-studies/)). This is relevant to any design that treats user-reported order as evidence.
- **Predictive lead-lag methods do not by themselves establish medical causation.** Granger causality is a predictive time-series framework; a review by Shojaie and Fox notes that its validity for inferring causal relationships remains debated and that historical implementations have often been limited to bivariate or pairwise vector autoregressive settings ([Shojaie and Fox, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02675)). A neuroscience review describes Granger causality as a statistical, predictive notion where putative causes precede and improve prediction of effects, while noting assumptions and interpretation limits ([Seth, Barrett, and Barnett, 2015](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/35/8/3293)).

### 3. Single-subject, n-of-1, and self-matched pattern methods

- **N-of-1 trial reporting standards.** CENT 2015 extends CONSORT for individual and series N-of-1 trials and includes guidance for reporting individual N-of-1 trials, diagrams, and modified flow diagrams ([CENT 2015 BMJ statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h1738)). N-of-1 standards are relevant because the product analyzes one individual's longitudinal data, but formal N-of-1 trials usually assume planned interventions, cycles, and often stronger controls than passive personal health graphs.
- **Single-case experimental design (SCED) standards and visual analysis.** The What Works Clearinghouse single-case design standards distinguish design standards from evidence standards and use visual analysis categories such as strong, moderate, or no evidence for a relation ([WWC Single-Case Design Technical Documentation](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Document/229)). A systematic visual-analysis protocol paper summarizes WWC visual analysis as considering level, trend, variability, immediacy, overlap, and consistency ([Ledford et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745757/)). These concepts provide external pattern vocabulary for repeated within-person signals without forcing causal claims.
- **Dynamic regression and time-series methods for n-of-1 data.** Vieira et al. state that n-of-1 analyses require modeling outcome distribution, time trends, autocorrelation/error structures, and contextualized effect sizes, while noting that no consensus exists on the best method for each n-of-1 design ([Vieira et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28629262/)).
- **Self-controlled designs reduce some confounding but have scope limits.** The self-controlled case series method uses individuals as their own controls and eliminates time-invariant confounding, but assumes conditions appropriate to transient exposures and events and does not automatically solve time-varying confounding ([Petersen, Douglas, and Whitaker, 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/354/bmj.i4515)). Case-crossover designs compare exposure during a hazard window with control windows in the same person and are used for transient exposure/acute event questions; they are limited for cumulative, long-term effects and can be challenged by time-varying confounding and co-occurring exposures ([BMJ Medicine: Case-crossover design for sudden events](https://bmjmedicine.bmj.com/content/1/1/e000214)).
- **Intensive longitudinal and ecological momentary assessment methods reduce but do not remove recall and burden issues.** EMA repeatedly samples behaviors or states in real time or near-real time; reviews describe it as repeated, real-time sampling of health behaviors in context, while noting adherence and burden concerns ([Perski et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9704370/); [van Berkel et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8867293/)).

### 4. Responsible association reporting, confounding, and multiplicity controls

- **Observational association reporting standards.** STROBE was created to improve reporting of cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional observational studies; it states that readers need to know what was planned, done, found, and what results mean, but also that the checklist is not a design prescription or quality-evaluation instrument ([STROBE Statement](https://www.strobe-statement.org/)).
- **Association is not causation; confounding and reverse causation remain live alternatives.** A statistical note on observational studies explicitly distinguishes association from causation and situates pattern identification and statistical association near, but not equivalent to, causal inference ([Donnelly et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8882362/)). Broader epidemiology materials likewise require chance, bias, and confounding to be considered before causal interpretation ([Health Knowledge: Causation in Epidemiology](https://www.healthknowledge.org.uk/e-learning/epidemiology/practitioners/causation-epidemiology-association-causation)).
- **Multiple testing controls address spurious patterns.** Benjamini and Hochberg introduced controlling the false discovery rate: the expected proportion of falsely rejected hypotheses among rejected hypotheses, an alternative to familywise error rate control ([Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/57/1/289/7035855)). The ASA statement on p-values warns that scientific conclusions should not be based only on whether a p-value crosses a threshold and that p-values do not measure effect size or importance ([ASA statement announcement](https://magazine.amstat.org/blog/2016/03/07/pvalue-mar16/); [ASA p-value statement PDF](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf)).

### 5. Confidence, uncertainty, and non-diagnostic communication patterns

- **Evidence certainty tiers.** GRADE uses high, moderate, low, and very low certainty categories to describe confidence in effect estimates; CDC's ACIP GRADE handbook states that certainty reflects confidence in effect estimates that help inform recommendations and distinguishes certainty from recommendation strength ([CDC ACIP GRADE Chapter 7](https://www.cdc.gov/acip-grade-handbook/hcp/chapter-7-grade-criteria-determining-certainty-of-evidence/index.html); [CDC ACIP GRADE Chapter 10](https://www.cdc.gov/acip-grade-handbook/hcp/chapter-10-overall-certainty-of-evidence/index.html)). This pattern is relevant as a model for evidence-strength language, but GRADE is built for bodies of evidence and guideline recommendations, not personal co-occurrence annotations.
- **Calibrated uncertainty language.** IPCC uncertainty guidance is an example of separating evidence/agreement-based confidence from likelihood/probability language and using calibrated terms consistently across authors ([IPCC uncertainty guidance page](https://www.ipcc.ch/publication/ipcc-cross-working-group-meeting-on-consistent-treatment-of-uncertainties/)).
- **Patient-facing risk communication.** Recent clinical risk-communication guidance summarizes practices including using numbers rather than only words, reducing cognitive effort, explaining what numbers mean, acknowledging uncertainty, and testing understanding ([Communicating Numeric Risk Information to Patients, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-025-09520-8)). Patient decision-aid guidance discusses denominators, time periods, consistent formats, and avoiding accidental nudging when presenting probabilities ([Bonner et al., 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0272989X21996328?download=true)). A systematic review of health probability communication finds mixed support for specific formats and states that no single visual or numerical format is optimal for every probability-communication situation ([Making Numbers Meaningful systematic review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12236432/)).
- **Uncertainty in self-generated or multi-source health data.** Research on patient-generated health data identifies accuracy/reliability, completeness, context, patient motivation, and representation as quality concerns for clinician use ([West et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701635/)). A remote monitoring review identifies PGHD management and quality challenges for clinical use ([Abdolkhani et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993998/)). An HCI study on multi-source mental-health data proposes uncertainty typologies and visualization design solutions for dynamic mood data ([Uncertainty in Multi-Source Mental Health Data, CHI 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3491101.3519844)).
- **Transparency expectations in decision support.** FDA's current Clinical Decision Support Software guidance page says the guidance addresses Non-Device CDS criteria and states that FDA policies still apply to device software, including software intended for patients or caregivers ([FDA CDS guidance, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software)). FDA's CDS FAQ summarizes Non-Device CDS criteria for HCP-facing tools, including providing recommendations/options rather than a specific directive and providing the basis so the HCP does not rely primarily on the recommendation; it also lists plain-language purpose, medical input, underlying algorithm, patient-specific information, and knowns/unknowns as examples relevant to basis transparency ([FDA CDS FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs)).

## Decision-neutral findings

### Decision question 1: Temporal precedence

**What external sources distinguish:**

- FHIR's separation of `effective[x]` from `issued` creates a precedent for separating the time an event is physiologically or clinically relevant from the time a record/result becomes available ([HL7 FHIR Observation R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/observation.html)). This distinction maps to WellBe's recorded timestamp vs event/onset timestamp question for C4 extraction and C6 temporal edges.
- Provenance standards frame timestamp interpretation as source-context-dependent. FHIR Provenance and W3C PROV-O model who/what created or influenced a record and what process produced it, which can support reliability assessment but does not convert temporal order into causality ([HL7 FHIR Provenance R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/provenance.html); [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)).
- EHR data-quality literature treats currency/timeliness, completeness, correctness, plausibility, conformance, and concordance as separate quality dimensions; it also emphasizes task dependence and variability across clinical datasets ([Weiskopf and Weng, 2013](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/20/1/144/2909176); [Kahn et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27713905/)). This supports treating timestamp order as one evidence input rather than a universally reliable ordering source.
- User-reported onset/order can add temporal evidence but carries recall-bias and recall-accuracy failure modes. Coughlin notes that recall accuracy varies by exposure and respondent characteristics, and that questionnaire/interviewing design affects recall quality ([Coughlin, 1990](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/recall-bias-in-epidemiologic-studies/)).
- Epidemiology treats temporality as necessary for causal inference, but not sufficient. Health Knowledge's causation chapter states that exposure must precede outcome while also noting chance, bias, confounding, and difficulty ascertaining sequence ([Health Knowledge: Causation in Epidemiology](https://www.healthknowledge.org.uk/e-learning/epidemiology/practitioners/causation-epidemiology-association-causation)).
- Granger-style lead-lag reasoning is predictive rather than clinical-causal. Sources describe Granger causality as causes preceding and improving prediction of effects, but with debated causal validity and assumptions/limitations ([Shojaie and Fox, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02675); [Seth, Barrett, and Barnett, 2015](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/35/8/3293)).

**Component mapping:**

- **C4 Processing Pipeline:** external patterns support extracting multiple time fields or confidence signals: record creation time, user-reported onset/order, source/result availability time, clinically relevant interval, missing/ambiguous temporal fields, and provenance.
- **C6 Knowledge Graph Store:** external patterns support representing temporal precedence as an evidence-weighted annotation over graph facts rather than a definitive causal assertion. Temporal uncertainty, source provenance, and conflicts between timestamps and reports are graph-level evidence issues.
- **C8 Pattern Memory:** external patterns support preserving user-readable caveats such as "recorded after," "reported as starting before," "exact order unclear," or "time window overlaps," while leaving final wording/policy undecided.

### Decision question 2: Co-occurrence / pattern definition

**What external sources distinguish:**

- Observational epidemiology treats an observed association as requiring evaluation against chance, bias, confounding, and alternative explanations before causal interpretation ([Health Knowledge: Causation in Epidemiology](https://www.healthknowledge.org.uk/e-learning/epidemiology/practitioners/causation-epidemiology-association-causation); [Donnelly et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8882362/)). In WellBe terms, co-occurrence is not by itself a disease claim, cause claim, or diagnosis.
- STROBE is a reporting pattern: it asks authors to disclose planned methods, what was done, what was found, and what results mean, while warning that its checklist is not a design prescription or quality instrument ([STROBE Statement](https://www.strobe-statement.org/)). For a product pattern view, this provides a transparency analogue: a surfaced pattern can separate method, observations, and caveats.
- Single-case design methods distinguish repeated signal assessment from one-off coincidence. WWC standards separate design validity from evidence strength and apply evidence categories only after design standards are met ([WWC Single-Case Design Technical Documentation](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Document/229)). Visual-analysis protocols examine level, trend, variability, immediacy, overlap, and consistency ([Ledford et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745757/)). These concepts can inform what makes a within-person co-occurrence look stronger or weaker, without requiring diagnosis.
- N-of-1 trial standards formalize stronger single-person evidence through planned, repeated, often randomized and controlled treatment periods ([CENT 2015 BMJ statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h1738)). Passive personal graph data generally lacks those safeguards, which affects how strongly a pattern can be framed.
- N-of-1 dynamic modeling literature identifies statistical issues that matter for repeated personal data: outcome distribution, time trend, autocorrelation/error structure, and contextualized effect size; it also notes that no single method is agreed upon for all n-of-1 designs ([Vieira et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28629262/)).
- Self-matched epidemiologic designs can reduce time-invariant confounding by comparing a person to themself, but their use cases are constrained. SCCS is framed around transient exposures and events and eliminates time-invariant confounding, while time-varying confounding remains a problem ([Petersen, Douglas, and Whitaker, 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/354/bmj.i4515)). Case-crossover designs are useful for acute trigger questions but are limited for cumulative effects and susceptible to time-varying confounding and co-occurring exposures ([BMJ Medicine: Case-crossover design for sudden events](https://bmjmedicine.bmj.com/content/1/1/e000214)).
- Multiple-hypothesis scanning increases the risk of spurious patterns. False discovery rate methods address the expected proportion of false discoveries among flagged hypotheses ([Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/57/1/289/7035855)), while the ASA warns against treating p-value thresholds as complete scientific conclusions or as measures of effect size/importance ([ASA p-value statement PDF](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf)).
- Patient-generated and wearable data introduce accuracy, completeness, context, and representation concerns that affect whether a repeated co-occurrence is interpretable ([West et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701635/); [Abdolkhani et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993998/)).

**Component mapping:**

- **C4 Processing Pipeline:** external methods suggest that candidate pattern features can include recurrence count, time-window definition, baseline frequency, trend, variability, missing-data rate, source agreement, temporal resolution, and potential confounder availability. This is a finding about available feature families, not a threshold recommendation.
- **C6 Knowledge Graph Store:** external methods support treating co-occurrence edges as evidence-weighted, source-linked annotations and preserving alternative explanations such as confounders, reverse order, common causes, seasonality, and missing data.
- **C8 Pattern Memory:** external methods support storing pattern narratives with evidence-for, evidence-against, missing-data, and caveat fields rather than a single causal label. Stronger single-person evidence externally tends to come from repeated, planned, controlled, or self-matched structures; weaker evidence comes from isolated, noisy, retrospective, or heavily missing observations.

### Decision question 3: Confidence / uncertainty scoring and surfacing

**What external sources distinguish:**

- GRADE separates evidence certainty from recommendation strength. CDC's GRADE handbook defines high/moderate/low/very low certainty as confidence in effect estimates that inform recommendations, and indicates that the lowest certainty among critical outcomes can determine overall certainty in some guideline contexts ([CDC ACIP GRADE Chapter 7](https://www.cdc.gov/acip-grade-handbook/hcp/chapter-7-grade-criteria-determining-certainty-of-evidence/index.html); [CDC ACIP GRADE Chapter 10](https://www.cdc.gov/acip-grade-handbook/hcp/chapter-10-overall-certainty-of-evidence/index.html)). This is relevant to evidence-strength semantics, but GRADE does not define confidence for personal exploratory patterns.
- IPCC uncertainty guidance is an external example of calibrated language that separates confidence from likelihood, but it is climate-assessment guidance rather than health-product UX guidance ([IPCC uncertainty guidance page](https://www.ipcc.ch/publication/ipcc-cross-working-group-meeting-on-consistent-treatment-of-uncertainties/)).
- Health risk-communication literature finds that words alone can be problematic. Numeric risk communication guidance identifies clinician reliance on verbal descriptions and low numeracy as challenges and lists best practices including using numbers, reducing cognitive effort, explaining the meaning of numbers, acknowledging uncertainty, and checking understanding ([Communicating Numeric Risk Information to Patients, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-025-09520-8)).
- Decision-aid guidance warns that probability formats can bias perception; it discusses denominators, time periods, consistent formats across outcomes/options, and avoiding accidental nudging ([Bonner et al., 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0272989X21996328?download=true)). A later systematic review states that no single visual or numerical probability format is optimal across all situations ([Making Numbers Meaningful systematic review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12236432/)).
- Multi-source/self-generated health data studies identify uncertainty dimensions that include source quality, completeness, context, and representation ([West et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5701635/); [Abdolkhani et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993998/); [CHI 2022 uncertainty visualization study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3491101.3519844)). These are inputs to a confidence explanation, not proofs of causal validity.
- FDA CDS materials emphasize transparency of basis, knowns/unknowns, and non-directive recommendations/options in HCP-facing Non-Device CDS summaries; FDA also says its existing digital health policies apply to device software, including patient/caregiver software ([FDA CDS guidance, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software); [FDA CDS FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs)). This is relevant to basis transparency and non-directive phrasing, not a definitive classification for WellBe.

**Component mapping:**

- **C4 Processing Pipeline:** confidence inputs supported by external evidence include data-quality dimensions, provenance quality, missingness, temporal precision, source agreement, autocorrelation/trend handling, multiple-testing exposure, and availability of confounder information.
- **C6 Knowledge Graph Store:** confidence can be treated as an evidence-weight over annotations or edges; external sources support separating strength of observation, uncertainty, provenance, and causal interpretation. They do not support converting correlation into a causal disease edge.
- **C8 Pattern Memory:** user-facing surfacing patterns in external sources include evidence-strength labels, explicit known/unknown lists, explanations of source basis, time-window language, and uncertainty caveats. External sources do not settle whether WellBe should use numeric percentages, tiers, badges, interval graphics, verbal labels, or combinations.

## Tradeoffs and open questions

### Temporal precedence options and risks

| Option space | Evidence basis | Main risk if used alone | Open questions for maintainers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recorded timestamp order | Available in most systems; aligns with record lineage and availability time concepts in FHIR `issued` ([HL7 FHIR Observation R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/observation.html)). | Can invert real-world order when records are entered late, imported in batches, corrected, or generated after a lab/result review. | Which timestamp types exist in C4 inputs, and how should record time be distinguished from event/onset/effective time? |
| Clinically relevant or source-provided event time | FHIR `effective[x]` models clinically relevant observation time; missing/indicative dates can be represented separately ([HL7 FHIR Observation R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/observation.html); [HL7 Europe missing data guidance](https://hl7.eu/fhir/base/missing-data.html)). | Source event times may be absent, approximate, copied forward, or semantically inconsistent across sources. | What temporal precision categories are needed: exact instant, date-only, interval, approximate period, relative order, unknown? |
| User-reported onset/order | Captures lived sequence that may not appear in records; relevant to personal-first graph semantics. | Recall bias and inaccurate recall can make retrospective order unreliable ([Coughlin, 1990](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/recall-bias-in-epidemiologic-studies/)). | Should user-reported order carry separate provenance and confidence from system timestamps? How should contradictions be shown? |
| Confidence-weighted combination | Data-quality and provenance literature supports task-dependent fitness and source lineage rather than a single universal field ([Weiskopf and Weng, 2013](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/20/1/144/2909176); [HL7 FHIR Provenance R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/provenance.html)). | A composite score can become opaque or appear more authoritative than the underlying evidence warrants. | Which inputs are explainable enough for users? Should the combination be rule-based, statistical, or qualitative? |
| Lead-lag predictive methods | Granger-style methods detect whether prior values improve prediction under assumptions ([Shojaie and Fox, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02675)). | Users may interpret predictive order as medical causation; assumptions may fail with sparse, irregular, confounded personal data. | Are lead-lag methods allowed only as internal candidate-generation signals, or can they be surfaced with caveats? |

### Pattern-definition options and risks

| Option space | Evidence basis | Main risk if used alone | Open questions for maintainers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple co-occurrence within a time window | Easy to compute and explain; aligns with exploratory Connect -> Investigate use. | High false-positive risk from chance, multiple comparisons, missingness, or common causes; association may be read as cause. | What minimum recurrence, time-window transparency, and caveat fields are required before surfacing? |
| Repeated within-person pattern with baseline comparison | SCED visual analysis looks at level, trend, variability, immediacy, overlap, and consistency ([Ledford et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6745757/)). | Passive personal data may not have clean phases or stable baselines. | Which SCED-like features are meaningful for observational graph data? |
| Statistical association with multiple-testing controls | FDR controls expected false discovery proportion when many hypotheses are tested ([Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/57/1/289/7035855)). | Statistical significance can be mistaken for importance, reliability, or causation; p-values alone are insufficient ([ASA p-value statement PDF](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf)). | Should statistical flags be hidden, translated to evidence language, or shown with explanation? |
| Self-matched trigger-style analysis | SCCS and case-crossover designs reduce time-invariant confounding in certain transient exposure/event contexts ([Petersen, Douglas, and Whitaker, 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/354/bmj.i4515); [BMJ Medicine case-crossover](https://bmjmedicine.bmj.com/content/1/1/e000214)). | Not suitable for slow, cumulative, chronic, or bidirectional relationships; time-varying confounding remains. | Which pattern classes are eligible for self-matched analysis, and which are explicitly excluded? |
| N-of-1 trial-like evidence | CENT and n-of-1 methods provide stronger individual evidence when design controls exist ([CENT 2015 BMJ statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/350/bmj.h1738); [Vieira et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28629262/)). | Most user graph data is not randomized, blinded, or prospectively planned; trial language could overstate rigor. | Should WellBe distinguish passive observational patterns from user/clinician-planned experiments or theories? |
| Confounder-aware candidate surfacing | Epidemiology requires chance, bias, and confounding to be considered before causal interpretation ([Health Knowledge](https://www.healthknowledge.org.uk/e-learning/epidemiology/practitioners/causation-epidemiology-association-causation)). | Over-suppressing patterns until confounders are modeled may hide useful user questions; under-suppressing may imply cause. | Which confounders are required to display, which can be missing-data prompts, and how should unknown confounding be worded? |

### Confidence and uncertainty-surfacing options and risks

| Option space | Evidence basis | Main risk if used alone | Open questions for maintainers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualitative tiers such as high/moderate/low evidence | GRADE and WWC use tiered evidence-strength categories in their own domains ([CDC ACIP GRADE Chapter 7](https://www.cdc.gov/acip-grade-handbook/hcp/chapter-7-grade-criteria-determining-certainty-of-evidence/index.html); [WWC SCD](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Document/229)). | A tier can look like diagnostic certainty unless tied to "evidence for this observed pattern," not disease truth. | What exact noun should the tier modify: confidence in timing, recurrence, source quality, association, or may-explain edge? |
| Numeric score or percentage | Numeric risk communication can improve clarity when denominators, time periods, and meaning are explained ([Communicating Numeric Risk Information to Patients, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-025-09520-8)). | A percentage may be read as probability of a cause or diagnosis; false precision is likely if the score is heuristic. | Is any numeric score calibrated? What denominator and interpretation can be truthfully shown? |
| Evidence checklist / knowns and unknowns | FDA CDS FAQ highlights basis transparency and knowns/unknowns for non-device CDS summaries ([FDA CDS FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs)). | Checklists may become lengthy or shift cognitive burden to the user. | Which evidence fields must be visible by default versus expandable? |
| Source-linked explanation with provenance | FHIR Provenance and W3C PROV support source lineage and reliability assessment ([HL7 FHIR Provenance R5](https://fhir.hl7.org/fhir/provenance.html); [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). | Source linkage can be mistaken for truth validation if provenance is conflated with medical correctness. | How should the UI distinguish "we know where this came from" from "this is medically true"? |
| Visual uncertainty cues | HCI work on multi-source mental-health data explores uncertainty typologies and visualization design solutions ([CHI 2022 uncertainty visualization study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3491101.3519844)). | Visual cues may be misunderstood or ignored; overly salient warnings may alarm users. | Which visual formats match WellBe's calm, non-alarm identity and are understandable to lay users? |
| Verbal probability/confidence terms | IPCC uses calibrated language; health communication literature notes verbal terms can be challenging and context-dependent ([IPCC uncertainty guidance page](https://www.ipcc.ch/publication/ipcc-cross-working-group-meeting-on-consistent-treatment-of-uncertainties/); [Making Numbers Meaningful systematic review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12236432/)). | Words like "likely," "strong," or "possible" may carry different meanings for different users and may imply causation. | Should any verbal labels be calibrated internally, user-tested, or paired with definitions? |

### Cross-cutting open questions

- **Unit of confidence:** Sources distinguish confidence in data quality, timestamp accuracy, statistical association, evidence certainty, and causal interpretation. WellBe must decide which of these is being scored or surfaced for each pattern annotation.
- **Minimum evidence to surface:** External methods provide feature families and caveats, but they do not define a universal threshold for a consumer personal health graph. The choice remains product- and risk-specific.
- **Confounder/missing-data treatment:** Sources support exposing missingness and considering confounders, but they do not determine whether missing confounder data should suppress surfacing, downgrade confidence, or appear as a missing-data prompt.
- **Pattern wording boundary:** Epidemiology and statistical sources support association/correlation caution, but product wording must define exactly where phrases such as "appears with," "often follows," "may be related to," and `may_explain` are allowed or disallowed.
- **Calibration and validation:** Numeric or tiered confidence displays can only be interpreted as calibrated probabilities if empirically calibrated. The sources reviewed provide communication patterns and analytic caveats, not a validated WellBe-specific score.
- **User control and correction:** Provenance and data-quality literature support source traceability, but product semantics must decide how user corrections update event order, edge confidence, Pattern Memory, and prior surfaced annotations.
- **Regulatory boundary:** FDA CDS materials are relevant to transparency and directive-vs-options framing, but they are not a substitute for legal/regulatory analysis of a patient-facing product that analyzes medical patterns.

## Approaches considered

Grounded only in the recorded research:

- **Temporal precedence.** FHIR separates `effective[x]` (clinically relevant time) from `issued` (availability time), supporting multiple time fields rather than one. EHR data-quality literature (Weiskopf & Weng; Kahn et al.) treats currency/completeness/plausibility as separate, task-dependent dimensions. User-reported onset/order carries recall-bias risk (Coughlin). Epidemiology (Bradford Hill via Health Knowledge) treats temporality as *necessary but not sufficient* for causation; Granger-style lead-lag is *predictive*, not clinical-causal (Shojaie & Fox; Seth et al.).
- **Pattern definition.** Observational association requires evaluation against chance/bias/confounding before any causal reading (Donnelly et al.). Single-case methods (WWC, Ledford et al.) supply within-person features — level, trend, variability, immediacy, overlap, consistency. N-of-1 (CENT) and self-matched designs (SCCS, case-crossover) give stronger but scope-limited single-person evidence. FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg) addresses multiple-comparison scanning; the ASA warns p-values are not effect size/importance.
- **Confidence/uncertainty.** GRADE/WWC use qualitative evidence-strength tiers; IPCC uses calibrated language separating confidence from likelihood. Risk-communication work shows no single numeric/visual format is universally best and that verbal terms are interpreted inconsistently. FDA CDS framing favors basis transparency and non-directive options.

## Decision

1. **Temporal precedence = evidence-weighted annotation, never a causal claim.** C4 extracts multiple time fields per fact (record/ingest time, source effective/event time, user-reported onset/order), each with its own provenance and a **temporal-precision category** (exact / date-only / interval / approximate / relative-order / unknown). C6 represents precedence as a source-linked, evidence-weighted edge annotation. Granger/lead-lag methods may run **only as internal candidate generation** and are never surfaced as causation. Conflicts between system timestamps and user-reported order are preserved and shown, not silently resolved.
2. **Pattern = a co-occurrence candidate with mandatory caveats, never a diagnosis.** A surfaced pattern carries recurrence count, a transparent time window, baseline frequency, trend/variability, missing-data rate, source agreement, and **explicit alternative explanations** (confounder, reverse order, common cause, seasonality, missing data). The strongest causal edge exposed remains `may_explain`. Multiple-comparison exposure is tracked internally; statistical flags (e.g., FDR) are translated to plain evidence language and never shown as bare p-values. Patterns live in C8 Pattern Memory with `evidence_for` / `evidence_against` / `missing_data` / `caveats` fields.
3. **Confidence = qualitative, source-linked evidence-strength tiers** that explicitly modify *"confidence in this observed pattern"* (timing/recurrence/source quality/association), **not** disease truth. No numeric percentage is shown unless empirically calibrated (none is). Each surfaced pattern shows: a plain-language evidence-strength label, a short knowns/unknowns line, source basis (one tap), and time-window/uncertainty caveats. Allowed wording: "appears with", "often follows", "may be related to"; **disallowed**: causal/diagnostic phrasing. The UI distinguishes "we know where this came from" (provenance) from "this is medically true".

## Trade-offs accepted

- **Qualitative tiers over numeric scores:** less sortable, but avoids false precision and uncalibrated probability claims.
- **Confounder-aware surfacing with mandatory caveats** rather than hard suppression: accepts that some weaker patterns surface, mitigated by required `evidence_against` and missing-data disclosure; the alternative (suppress until confounders modeled) would hide useful user questions.
- **User-reported order retained as separate-provenance evidence** despite recall-bias risk, clearly labeled as reported (preserves the personal-first lived sequence).

## Implementation notes

- **C4:** extract multi-time fields + precision categories + data-quality signals (completeness, concordance, currency).
- **C6:** store temporal and co-occurrence relations as evidence-weighted, source-linked annotations with alternative-explanation slots; `may_explain` is the ceiling.
- **C8:** store pattern narratives with evidence-for/against/missing/caveat structure.
- **C10:** reviews pattern wording for non-diagnostic compliance before surfacing; lead-lag outputs are internal-only.
- **Deferred (tracked):** minimum evidence threshold to surface, the required-confounder display list, and whether any verbal labels get user-tested — to implementation with safety review.
